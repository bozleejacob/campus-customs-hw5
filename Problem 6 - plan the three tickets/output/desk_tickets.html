Ticket 101 — customer_order

Requester: Tauhid Zaman
Subject: Bulldog tee
SKU / size / qty: CC-TEE-WHITE / S / 1
Linked invoice: 501
Notes: "Needs a tee in size S."
Expected

Who Boss calls first, and why

Inventory — this is a customer order for one specific SKU/size, so the very first question is whether we have it on hand at all. Everything else (restock, payment, customer messaging) only matters once we know the stock answer.

Expected delegation chain

  1. Boss → Inventory: "Check stock for CC-TEE-WHITE size S on ticket 101, and if it's short, find who can restock it and whether anything is blocking that vendor." Why: stock level decides whether this is a same-day fulfillment or a restock problem.
  2. Inventory → Accounting: "Invoice 501 to Bulldog Print Co looks tied to this SKU — is it open/unpaid, and can the shop afford to clear it so the vendor will ship?" Why: the shop rule is a vendor won't ship while they have an open unpaid invoice, and only Accounting can judge cash impact and prep a payment for human approval.
  3. Boss → Customer Service: "Draft a status update for Tauhid Zaman about the tee order." Why: once Boss has the stock/restock answer, the customer still needs to hear something — but it's a draft only, never sent automatically.

Expected MCP tools used

  • get_inventory_level(sku="CC-TEE-WHITE", size="S") Inventory confirms exact on-hand quantity before anything else.
  • get_vendor(vendor_id=1) Inventory identifies Bulldog Print Co as the apparel-reprint vendor who could restock this SKU.
  • get_invoice(invoice_id=501) Checks whether that vendor's invoice is open, since an open invoice blocks shipping.
  • get_cash_balance(account="checking") Accounting checks whether paying invoice 501 is affordable before recommending it.
Actual

What actually happened

Agents who worked this ticket: Boss, Inventory, Accounting, Customer Service. (Facilities was not needed.)

Actual delegation chain

  1. Boss → Inventory: "Verify on-hand quantity and location for CC-TEE-WHITE size S, qty 1. Also check whether invoice 501 affects fulfillment by confirming its current status; report facts only."
  2. Boss → Accounting: "Review invoice 501 and report its amount, due date, and current status. Advise whether any payment approval is needed before fulfillment." (Boss delegated to Inventory and Accounting directly and in parallel, rather than Inventory delegating on to Accounting as we predicted.)
  3. Boss → Customer Service: "Draft (do not send) a customer response to Tauhid Zaman... explain the tee is currently unavailable and avoid promising a restock or delivery date."

Actual MCP tools used

  • get_open_tickets(), get_desk_today(), get_sku_pricing(sku="CC-TEE-WHITE") called by Boss
  • get_inventory_level(sku="CC-TEE-WHITE", size="S") → qty 0, Aisle B called by Inventory
  • get_invoice(invoice_id=501) → open, $840, due 2026-08-28 called by both Inventory and Accounting
  • get_cash_balance(account="checking"), get_all_tickets() called by Accounting

Note: get_vendor was never actually called — both agents resolved the "is it blocked" question straight from the invoice status, so the vendor lookup we expected turned out to be unnecessary.

Outcome

Boss: "Ticket 101 cannot currently be fulfilled" — 0 on hand, invoice 501 open/unpaid ($840) blocking the vendor's restock, customer draft prepared. Human approval: Jake approved payment of $840.00 against invoice 501 (checking account) — invoice marked paid, balance 3,400.00 → 2,560.00.

Ticket 102 — rent_notice

Requester: Elm City Properties
Subject: Rent due
Linked lease: 1 (Chapel Street shop)
Notes: "Email: shop rent due in 2 days."
Expected

Who Boss calls first, and why

Facilities — this is a lease/rent ticket, and Facilities is the only role that owns lease terms and due dates. It needs to confirm the real due date and compare it against today before anyone talks about money.

Expected delegation chain

  1. Boss → Facilities: "Confirm lease 1's rent amount and due date, and how urgent this is against today's date." Why: the notice says "2 days," but only the lease record and desk date are authoritative — never take the notice's own framing at face value.
  2. Facilities → Accounting: "Rent of $2,400 is due 2026-09-02 for lease 1 — do we have the cash, and prep the payment for human approval." Why: once urgency is confirmed, only Accounting can check the account balance and get a payment ready for a human to approve.

No Customer Service step expected here — the landlord isn't a customer, and the shop doesn't draft or send anything to real vendors/landlords; this stays an internal cash decision for Boss to finalize.

Expected MCP tools used

  • get_lease_details(lease_id=1) Facilities gets the authoritative rent amount and next_due date.
  • get_desk_today() Facilities compares next_due against the shop's actual "today" to judge urgency.
  • get_cash_balance(account="checking") Accounting checks whether $2,400 is affordable right now.
Actual

What actually happened

Agents who worked this ticket: Boss, Accounting, Facilities. (Customer Service was not needed — matches our prediction that the landlord isn't a customer.)

Reflection — Problem 10

1. How would I evaluate the agent's performance on each ticket?

Across all three tickets, I'd call the performance solid: nothing was invented, every number in every final answer traces back to a real tool call, and no agent ever tried to pay anything on its own — the guardrail I put in place (pulling record_payment out of every agent's toolset) actually held up under a real run, not just in theory.

Ticket 101 was the cleanest "catch." The agent correctly figured out the tee was out of stock and that the real blocker wasn't inventory, it was an unpaid vendor invoice sitting in the way of a restock. That's a two-hop piece of reasoning (0 in stock → why can't we just reorder → because the vendor won't ship with an open bill) and it got there without me hinting at it.

Ticket 102 got the right answer (pay the $2,400 rent, pending approval) but took a noticeably chattier path to get there — Accounting ended up delegating to both Facilities and back to Boss, re-confirming the same lease facts more than once. It even caught a subtle detail I wouldn't have thought to ask it to check: the cash balance's "as of" date had drifted to today's real date while the shop's "today" was still August 31, and it flagged that mismatch instead of quietly papering over it. Good catch, wasteful route.

Ticket 103 was the sharpest one. It didn't even bother looping in Accounting for a margin calculation, because the real blocker (only 8 of 20 hoodies in stock) made the discount question moot — you can't discount units you don't have. That's the kind of judgment call I was hoping to see out of a "Boss," not just mechanical tool-chaining.

2. For each ticket, how did actual compare to expected?

101: The outcome matched my prediction (blocked on the invoice, needs human payment approval), but the shape of the delegation didn't. I expected Inventory to check stock first and then hand the invoice question to Accounting. What actually happened is Boss fired off both delegations in parallel itself, instead of letting Inventory pass it along. get_vendor, which I expected to get called, never was — both agents just answered from the invoice record directly.

102: This is the one that diverged the most. I predicted a tidy Boss → Facilities → Accounting chain. What I got was Boss → Accounting first, and then Accounting fanning out to both Facilities and back to Boss. The final recommendation was basically what I expected, just reached through a loopier, more "full-connectivity" route than I'd planned on paper.

103: This one matched almost exactly — Inventory first, then a Customer Service draft — except Accounting never got pulled in at all. I'd predicted Inventory would hand the margin question to Accounting; instead Inventory just pulled the pricing itself, and Boss skipped the margin conversation entirely since the quantity shortfall already killed the discount idea.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why?

Honestly, for exactly these three tickets, I think a single "Shop Ops" agent with all nine MCP tools would have worked just as well, and been cheaper and faster. Looking back at the actual tool calls, a lot of the delegation was agents re-fetching facts another agent had already pulled — ticket 102 alone called get_lease_details three separate times across Boss, Accounting, and Facilities, for the same lease, in the same run. None of that repetition changed the answer; it just burned tokens and round-trips.

The five-role split really earns its keep when you want hard boundaries around authority, not just division of labor — keeping payment power quarantined to Accounting-plus-a-human, and keeping customer-facing language quarantined to Customer Service, so a stock-checking bug can never accidentally also be the thing that emails a customer or pays a bill. For three tickets and nine tools, a single agent with a clear step-by-step prompt ("check stock → check invoice/vendor → check cash → decide → draft only if it's customer-facing → report") would have reached the same three outcomes with noticeably less back-and-forth. The multi-agent design is an investment that pays off more as the shop and the tool count grow, not necessarily at this scale.

4. Three new problems the current team + tools could solve

  • A new rent notice that's actually overdue, not just upcoming. We already have get_lease_details + get_desk_today + record_payment wired through Facilities and Accounting — a ticket where the due date has already passed would run through basically the same chain we just watched, just with more urgency in Boss's final call.
  • A new vendor invoice for a different SKU reprint or restock. Ticket 101 proved out the whole invoice-blocks-shipment pattern end to end (Inventory + Accounting + get_invoice + get_vendor + human-approved record_payment). Any new "customer wants an out-of-stock item and there's an open vendor bill" ticket is the same shape.
  • A bulk-discount request where there's actually enough stock. We built the whole margin-check path (Inventory checks quantity, Accounting pulls get_sku_pricing to compare unit cost vs. list price) but ticket 103 never actually exercised it, since the shortfall made it moot. A future "20 hoodies, and we have 20 hoodies" ticket would be the first real test of that discount-approval logic.

5. Three new problems the current team + tools could NOT solve, and what I'd need to add

  • Proactively restocking before a vendor invoice even exists. Every tool we have assumes the invoice already exists and Accounting just pays it. There's no way for Inventory to actually place a new order with a vendor. I'd need a new MCP tool like create_purchase_order(vendor_id, sku, qty) that writes a new invoice (or a dedicated purchase_orders table), and probably a "Procurement" responsibility — either a new agent or an expansion of Inventory's authority — still gated behind the same human-approval rule before any money moves.
  • A customer return or refund. Our whole money model is one-directional — the assignment explicitly says cash only leaves the shop, nothing comes back in. A real shop needs to handle "this hoodie was defective, refund the customer," which doesn't fit record_payment at all (it debits cash_accounts, it doesn't credit it from an external source). I'd need a returns table, a new tool to log and adjust for a refund, and a rule change allowing inbound cash in a very narrow, audited way.
  • Comparing multiple vendors to find the best price before restocking. Right now the vendors table has exactly one vendor per specialty, so there's never actually a choice to make. A ticket like "find the cheapest vendor to reprint 50 tees" needs a vendor_quotes (or similar) table and a tool to pull and compare quotes across vendors — and probably a more analytical "Procurement/Sourcing" agent role whose whole job is comparing options, which is a different kind of thinking than anything Inventory or Accounting does today.
